Operating Thesis

I build the planning, cadence, and analytics infrastructure that turns sales strategy into seller productivity and predictable bookings.

I don’t believe most sales organizations miss their number because of bad strategy. They miss because the operating systems underneath the strategy fragment as the business scales.

Reps optimize for the quarter. Managers optimize for forecast hygiene. Marketing optimizes for funnel volume. Finance optimizes for variance. Each function performs rationally inside its own domain, yet the revenue system as a whole loses coherence as complexity compounds.

My work has consistently focused on solving that fragmentation problem.

Across enterprise solution sales, strategic account leadership, and SMB commercial operations spanning telecom, enterprise technology, and GovTech, I’ve specialized in building sales operating models that connect strategy to execution through annual planning rigor, territory and capacity architecture, forecast governance, pipeline inspection cadence, and the systems and data infrastructure that hold sellers and managers accountable to the number.

The objective is not better dashboards in isolation. The objective is operational alignment — creating a sales system where Marketing, SDR, Field Sales, Customer Success, Finance, and Partners execute against an operating rhythm capable of scaling consistently as organizational complexity increases.

Forecasting, in my view, should not be a reporting exercise — it should be a managerial inspection discipline that surfaces risk early and drives weekly decisions. Pipeline should not be a static dashboard — it should be a system of signals tied to coverage targets, stage conversion benchmarks, and deal-cycle velocity. Capacity and quota should not be a top-down allocation exercise — they should be bottoms-up models grounded in territory potential, productivity assumptions, and ramp economics. AI should not exist as isolated experimentation — it should be embedded infrastructure that compresses friction across the revenue lifecycle.

As sales organizations scale across segments, geographies, and channels simultaneously, this operating philosophy increasingly shaped how I designed sales operating cadences and the systems that hold them together.

The sales organizations that outperform over time are rarely the ones with the most aggressive targets. They are the ones that build the strongest sales operating systems underneath them.

That has been the central thread of my career.
